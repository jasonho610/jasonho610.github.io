Kuan-Hsun Jason Ho

What musicians know about sound can teach audio models.

Audio DSP and ML. Jazz Saxophonist.

I work on audio machine learning and signal processing, and I play tenor saxophone.

The two keep meeting. Musicians spend years learning which differences in sound matter — which take is better, which frequency is fighting the vocal. I want audio models to learn the same things, and I want to be able to check whether they really did.

I started with DSP: a loudness meter in C++ at NTPU. Then speech enhancement for my master’s at NTNU, two years writing real-time effects at Positive Grid, and now MixJudge, a model you can ask in plain words what a vocal mix needs.

I am applying for PhD study in music technology, starting autumn 2027.

Building

Teaching models musical ideas. MixJudge learns eleven things that commonly go wrong in a vocal mix, so you can ask it what a mix needs and it can rank how badly it needs it.

Understanding

Checking what a model actually uses. I rebuilt a speech model so you can simply read off which frequency bands it leans on, instead of guessing from a score.

Playing along

The long-term goal: an accompanist that listens. One that keeps the groove, follows the energy, leaves space, and answers a phrase when answering is the right call.

Worth a look
