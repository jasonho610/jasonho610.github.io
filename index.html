What does a model actually listen to?

Audio DSP and ML. Jazz Saxophonist.

six bandpass filters, an octave apart — run your cursor across them

I am an audio engineer and a jazz saxophonist, and I have never managed to keep the two apart.

On a bandstand you learn to hear one voice inside an arrangement and decide, in about half a second, whether it is working. That habit is what I bring to signal processing and machine learning — and it is why the question above keeps pulling me back. A loss value tells you whether a model worked. It does not tell you what the model used, or whether its judgment is one a musician would recognise.

So I build things that have to answer to an ear: effects that ship inside instruments people play, and models that can say which part of a sound they are responding to. Next I want to do that full time, as a PhD in music information retrieval.

Listening

I research machine listening for music and speech. Lately that means a critic you can ask, in plain language, what is wrong with a vocal mix. Before that, a few years teaching speech models to show which frequency bands they actually rely on — published at ICASSP, Interspeech, MLSP and SLT.

Building

Two years writing the real-time DSP inside guitar software and smart amps at Positive Grid: effects, neural amp models squeezed onto microcontrollers, and a great deal of replaying the same four bars until they sounded right. Shipping teaches what papers cannot — a real-time thread has no patience.

Playing

Lead tenor in a Taipei big band, a regular at Blue Note Taipei, and a solo on a record nominated for Best Jazz Album at the Golden Indie Music Awards. The playing is not a hobby sitting beside the work. It is where the questions come from.

If you only open one thing

  • MixJudge A critic that can be asked, in words, what a vocal mix needs — and rank how badly it needs it.
  • What Do Neural Networks Listen To? Rebuilding a speech model so its frequency selectivity is something you can simply read off. ICASSP 2024.
  • A loudness meter Open-source LKFS and true-peak metering in modern C++, written to the ITU-R standard.
